# Two real projects, anonymised

The framework was built and tested on two client sites. Names, numbers and wording are removed; the lessons are not.

## Project A: a US marketing agency (WordPress rebuild, ~25 pages, 8 case studies)

| Stage | What happened | What it taught |
|---|---|---|
| Truth | The live-site audit found the agency's real strength hidden on orphan landing pages. 3 of 48 images were filed under the wrong client; 17 were stock or unused. | Crawl orphan pages. Open every image. |
| Story | Three rounds of polished headlines were rejected: *"these options don't define the business."* The fourth came from the agency's own engagement notes: how clients arrived, what they bought next, why they stayed. | Positioning lives in the business arc, not in taglines. |
| System | The first design (cream, serif italics, mono labels) was rejected twice as looking AI-made. The hero (photo + dashboard + phone + badge) was called cluttered. Pages repeated a process block nine times. Sections drifted off the grid on wide screens. | The anti-AI-look list, the calm-hero rule, one idea per page, one layout frame checked at four widths. |
| System, rebrand | The client asked whether its long-time orange was really right. Four directions were compared on story, distinctiveness, retainer-readiness, client work and continuity. The winner came from textures in the agency's own culture photography. | Build the palette from the client's own world, and check it against the colours of the work the site will show. |
| Ship | Editor validation, a full crawl and a fresh rebuild from files in about two minutes. A heading said "Four" above three cards. | Automate what can be automated; read every page as a buyer. |

## Project B: a UK car care brand (marketing site, no checkout)

| Stage | What happened | What it taught |
|---|---|---|
| Setup | The starter was agency-shaped (services, case studies, strategy call). | `--kind product-brand`: product lines, customer builds, "Where to buy" and dealer enquiries. |
| System | The new site first came out wearing Project A's design with new colours. Rejected: *"every design should be different, based on the raw data from the client."* | The base became a wireframe with no design; stage 3 designs each client from raw data (geography, buyer, category, price, their own world). |
| System | Brief: UK/Europe, enthusiasts and detailers, mid-market. Three directions; chosen: British racing green with an aqua "bead" (water beading), wide livery capitals, technical corners. Fonts self-hosted for GDPR. | Design from the brief, then verify it against the competitor teardown. |
| Truth | A teardown of four competitors (colours read from their CSS) found one rival already used a teal close to the planned accent. | Read real competitor CSS; don't trust assumptions about a category's look. |
